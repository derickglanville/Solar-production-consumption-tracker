Why the NYSEG credit bank does not show a growing credit since July 16

The July 2–August 5 statement spans the smart-meter transition. It records 1,025 kWh exported, but it does not itemize an official excess-generation credit balance. Because the tracker began after the billing period had already started, there is no matching opening M02 reading to reconcile that period.

The corrected September 16 statement does show NYSEG’s treatment: it applied credit against 518 kWh of billed use. Its excess-generation table lists 1,376 kWh prior excess and 0 kWh remaining after the correction. That means the bill used the accumulated excess to offset energy use instead of carrying a visible kWh bank forward. The remaining $43.57 in energy-related charges were fixed or non-bypassable items, not a charge for the 518 kWh of use.

This report can show when NYSEG’s documented treatment differs from M02. It does not assume every exported kWh becomes a one-for-one dollar credit unless the statement explicitly says so.
