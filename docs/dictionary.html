What Is a Kilowatt-Hour (kWh)?

A kilowatt-hour (kWh) is a measure of energy, not power.

The easiest way to think about it is:

  • Kilowatt (kW) = how fast electricity is being used or produced (like the speed of a car).
  • Kilowatt-hour (kWh) = the total amount of electricity used or produced over time (like the distance the car travels).

The definition

A 1 kWh is the amount of energy used by a 1,000-watt (1 kW) device running for 1 hour.

Examples:

  • A 1,000-watt microwave running for 1 hour = 1 kWh
  • A 100-watt light bulb running for 10 hours = 1 kWh
  • A 2,000-watt space heater running for 30 minutes = 1 kWh

Relating it to your solar system

Your system is rated at 18.45 kW.

That means:

If it produced its full rated power for one hour, it would generate: 18.45 kWh

Of course, the sun changes throughout the day, so your system doesn't produce 18.45 kW continuously.

For example, on an excellent summer day your system might generate: 75 kWh

That doesn't mean it ran at 18.45 kW all day. Instead, it may have produced something like:

TimeApproximate Power
7:00 AM1 kW
9:00 AM8 kW
11:00 AM15 kW
1:00 PM17 kW
3:00 PM14 kW
5:00 PM6 kW
7:00 PM1 kW

The area under that "power curve" over the course of the day is the total energy, about 75 kWh.

Relating it to your home

Before solar, your annual NYSEG usage was: 17,967 kWh/year

That averages to: 49.2 kWh/day

Think of it this way:

Every day your house uses about 49 "units" of electricity. Each of those units is one kWh.

Why your electric bill is in kWh

NYSEG charges you for energy, not power.

For example, if electricity costs $0.30 per kWh:

  • Using 10 kWh costs about $3.00
  • Using 50 kWh costs about $15.00
  • Using 1,000 kWh costs about $300.00

A water analogy

Imagine a garden hose:

  • kW = how fast the water is flowing (gallons per minute).
  • kWh = how many gallons of water filled the bucket.

The flow rate tells you how fast you're filling the bucket. The total gallons tell you how much water you actually collected.

That's exactly the relationship between kW and kWh.

For your solar system, the 18.45 kW rating tells you how much power it can produce at any instant under ideal conditions, while the 11,141 kWh/year guarantee tells you the total amount of electrical energy Sunrun expects it to generate over an entire year.

Solar Terms

Production

The total solar energy your system generated over a period, usually shown in kWh.

Irradiance

How intense the sunlight is at a moment in time, usually shown in watts per square meter (W/m²).

Export

Solar electricity that your house did not use immediately and sent back out to the grid.

Self-Consumption

Solar electricity that your home used directly instead of exporting it to the grid.

Billing Terms

Meter 01 Import

Electricity pulled from NYSEG into your home. Higher import means more grid dependence.

Meter 02 Export

Electricity pushed from your solar system back to NYSEG when production exceeds your home's immediate usage.

Fixed Charges

Recurring utility charges that usually remain even when solar production is strong.

Offset

How much of your home's electricity demand is being covered by solar instead of the grid.
